Phase-Plane Analysis of Excitability

one cell · ms

A model with two variables can be drawn completely in a plane, where every state is a point and the equations assign a direction of motion to each point. Here a fast voltage-like variable v is paired with a slow recovery variable w, and the curves along which each variable stands still, the nullclines, organise everything the neuron does.

Click anywhere in the plane to start the neuron from that state, and follow the same trajectory as a voltage trace on the right. Then raise the injected current, which lifts the cubic v-nullcline, and watch where the two nullclines cross.

Starting points close to rest fall straight back, while points beyond a narrow threshold region make a long excursion around the cubic before returning, and that excursion is the spike. When the current is high enough the resting state, the fixed point where the nullclines cross, loses its stability in a Hopf bifurcation, and the trajectory settles onto a limit cycle of repetitive firing.

Why does the threshold of this model look like a narrow band in the plane rather than a single voltage, and what does that imply for an experiment that measures threshold with current pulses?
Equations
dv/dt = v − v³/3 − w + I
dw/dt = ε (v + a − b w)
v-nullcline: w = v − v³/3 + I;   w-nullcline: w = (v + a)/b
ε = 0.08, a = 0.7, b = 0.8. Repetitive firing begins near I ≈ 0.33.
Pseudocode
choose a starting state (v, w)
for each time step dt:
    v ← v + dt · (v − v³/3 − w + I)
    w ← w + dt · ε · (v + a − b·w)
draw (v, w) in the plane, and v against time
