Laminar Cortical Microcircuit

≈ 0.5 mm across, 2 mm deep

The laminar microcircuit describes a column as eight populations, excitatory and inhibitory in each of four layers, connected by the pathways that dominate anatomical and physiological measurements: thalamic input into layer 4, layer 4 to layers 2/3, layers 2/3 to layer 5, layer 5 to layer 6, and layer 6 back to layer 4.

Set the strength of the thalamic input and watch activity move through the column on the left, while the right panel shows the rate of each layer's excitatory population. Cut one pathway and see which layers fall silent and which keep responding.

Cutting the pathway from layer 4 to layers 2/3 silences the superficial layers and everything downstream of them, while layer 4 itself keeps responding. The order of activation follows the wiring, which is why laminar recordings can be read as a map of the circuit.

Ask yourself
Layer 5 neurons also receive some thalamic input directly. How would the response to a cut between layers 4 and 2/3 change if that input were added?
Equations
τ drk/dt = −rk + f(Σl wlk rl + Ik(t)),   f(x) = max(0, tanh x)
Eight populations, E and I in L2/3, L4, L5 and L6. Cutting a pathway sets its weight wlk to zero.
Pseudocode
for each time step dt:
    for each population k:
        input_k ← Σ_l w_lk · r_l + thalamic input (L4E, weakly L6E)
        r_k ← r_k + dt/τ · (−r_k + f(input_k))
shade each layer by its excitatory rate
