Threshold and Reset

Reduced Neuron Models → Integrate-and-Fire Neuron

The integrate-and-fire neuron keeps the subthreshold voltage of a neuron and replaces the spike by a rule: when the voltage reaches a threshold, a spike is recorded and the voltage is reset. Repetitive firing is taken as given here, and the question is how much of it survives once the mechanism that generates each spike is removed.

The two-variable neuron of the previous plates and a leaky integrate-and-fire neuron receive the same current. In the integrate-and-fire trace each spike is drawn as a thin vertical tick, since the model computes no waveform. Change the current and the reset voltage, and compare the firing-rate curves on the right.

Spike times and firing rates survive the abstraction over a range of currents. The shape of the spike, its afterhyperpolarisation and the slow recovery are gone, and the reset voltage turns out to be a free choice that shifts the whole rate curve.

Equations
τ dV/dt = −(V − EL) + R I
if V ≥ θ: record a spike and set V ← Vreset
Integrate-and-fire parameters: τ = 8 ms, EL = −65 mV, θ = −55 mV, R = 1.5 mV per μA/cm², refractory period 5 ms. The two-variable model is stage 2 of Timescale Separation.
Pseudocode
for each time step dt:
    two-variable model: update V and n as in Timescale Separation
    integrate-and-fire: V ← V + dt/τ · (−(V − E_L) + R·I)
                        if V ≥ θ: draw a tick, V ← V_reset, hold for 5 ms
firing rate ← number of spikes / duration, for each current
