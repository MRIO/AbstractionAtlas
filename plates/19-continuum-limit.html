The Continuum Limit

Neural Mass Model → Neural Field

The continuum limit replaces a chain of discrete populations by a field defined at every point of the cortex. Connections between populations become a kernel w(x − y) that depends only on distance, and the sum over neighbours becomes an integral.

A ring of rate populations and the continuous field it approaches receive the same localised input. Change the number of populations and the width of the connectivity kernel, and compare the discrete steady state, drawn as stems, with the continuous profile.

With several populations within one kernel width the stems trace the continuous curve closely. When the kernel is narrower than the spacing between populations each population sees almost none of its neighbours, and the discrete chain no longer resembles the field.

Steady state: discrete ring and continuous field

Keeps
Spatial interactions, now expressed as a connectivity kernel.
Loses
The discreteness of columns and the specific pattern of long-range wiring.
Trust it when
Connectivity is local and roughly the same everywhere on the sheet.
Ask yourself
Why does a neural field so readily produce travelling waves, and what kind of connectivity would prevent them?
Equations
discrete: τ duk/dt = −uk + Δx Σl w(xk − xl) f(ul) + I(xk)
continuous: τ ∂u/∂t = −u + ∫ w(x − y) f(u(y, t)) dy + I(x)
the sum becomes the integral as Δx → 0;   w(x) = (w0/√(2π)σ) e−x²/2σ² − w1
Pseudocode
place M populations on a ring, spacing Δx = 2π/M
repeat until steady:
    for each population k:
        u_k ← u_k + dt/τ · (−u_k + Δx · Σ_l w(x_k − x_l)·f(u_l) + I(x_k))
do the same on a fine grid for the continuous field
draw the discrete values as stems over the continuous profile
