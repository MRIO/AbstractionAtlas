The Isopotential Approximation

Compartmental Neuron → Conductance-Based Point Neuron

The isopotential approximation treats the whole cell as one compartment with a single voltage. It is exact when the cell is electrotonically compact, short compared with its length constant, because then every point of the membrane sits at nearly the same potential.

The same synaptic current enters a passive cable and a single compartment with the same total membrane. Move the synapse and change the electrotonic length L/λ of the cell; the left panel overlays the two somatic voltages, and the right panel shows how far the point neuron's peak departs from the cable's for every synapse position.

For a compact cell, or for input close to the soma, the two traces coincide. For distal input on a long cell the point neuron overestimates the somatic response and misses its delay, and for input at the soma of a long cell it errs the other way, because it spreads every input evenly over the whole membrane at once.

Somatic voltage: cable and point neuron

Peak mismatch against electrotonic distance

Keeps
Spike generation, intrinsic currents and the excitability of the cell.
Loses
Dendritic integration, the location of synapses, backpropagating and dendritic spikes.
Trust it when
The cell is electrotonically compact, or the inputs of interest arrive close to the soma.
Ask yourself
Two identical synapses, one distal and one at the soma: what does the point neuron predict about their effects, and what does the cable predict?
Equations
cable: τ ∂V/∂t = λ² ∂²V/∂x² − V + rm isyn(x, t)
point neuron: τ dV/dt = −V + (1/N) Σk rm isyn,k(t)
electrotonic length L/λ;   electrotonic distance of the synapse X = x/λ
The point neuron has the total membrane of the N = 20 compartments, so a current that enters one compartment is spread over all of them.
Pseudocode
run the cable with input at compartment k
run one compartment:  V ← V + dt/τ · (−V + input/N)
compare V_soma(t) of the cable with V(t) of the point neuron
mismatch ← (peak_point − peak_cable) / peak_cable
repeat for every k to draw the mismatch curve
