The Forward Problem

Large-Scale Network Model → Forward Model of Measurement

The forward problem asks what a sensor records when a given source is active. A patch of synchronously active pyramidal cells acts as a current dipole, and the potential it produces on the scalp follows from the physics of volume conduction through brain, skull and scalp. For a given source and head model the answer is unique.

A dipole sits inside a head drawn as concentric shells. Change its depth and its orientation, from radial, pointing towards the scalp, to tangential, parallel to it, and read the potential along the scalp in the right panel.

Deep sources produce weak and widely smeared potentials, because distance and the poorly conducting skull blur them. Radial sources give a single peak above the source, while tangential sources give a pair of opposite peaks on either side of it; magnetoencephalography, unlike EEG, is nearly blind to radial sources.

Equations
φ(r) = (1/4πσ) p · (r − r0) / |r − r0|³
The potential of a current dipole p at r0 in an unbounded medium of conductivity σ. The scalp curve smooths this along the scalp to mimic the blurring by the skull; a full calculation would solve the problem for nested shells of different conductivity.
Pseudocode
place the dipole at depth d with orientation α
for each electrode on the scalp:
    φ ← p · (r_electrode − r_0) / |r_electrode − r_0|³
smooth φ along the scalp (skull blurring)
plot φ against electrode position
