Neural Mass Model of a Cortical Column

one column · 10 to 100 ms

This neural mass model describes a cortical column by three interacting populations, pyramidal cells with excitatory and inhibitory interneurons. Each connection passes the presynaptic firing rate through a synaptic kernel, fast for excitation and slow for inhibition, and a sigmoid turns the resulting mean membrane potential back into a rate.

The left panel draws the three populations with the impulse response of each kernel. The right panel shows the pyramidal potential, the model's stand-in for the EEG (and the LFP), with its power spectrum beneath it. Change the noise in the input and the ratio of excitatory to inhibitory gain.

At standard settings the model produces an alpha rhythm near 10 Hz. Raising the excitatory gain pushes it towards large, slow, spike-wave-like discharges, and lowering it quiets the rhythm until only filtered noise remains.

Equations
he(t) = A a t e−a t,   hi(t) = B b t e−b t
S(v) = 2e0 / (1 + er(v0 − v))
y0″ = A a S(y1 − y2) − 2a y0′ − a² y0
y1″ = A a [p(t) + C2 S(C1 y0)] − 2a y1′ − a² y1
y2″ = B b C4 S(C3 y0) − 2b y2′ − b² y2
A = 3.25 mV, B = 22 mV, a = 100 s⁻¹, b = 50 s⁻¹, C1 = 135, C2 = 108, C3 = C4 = 33.75, e0 = 2.5 s⁻¹, v0 = 6 mV, r = 0.56 mV⁻¹; input p(t) is noise around 220 s⁻¹. The gain slider sets A with B fixed.
Pseudocode
for each time step dt:
    rate into pyramidal cells ← S(y1 − y2)
    each kernel is a damped second-order filter:
        y'' ← gain·rate·k − 2k·y' − k²·y
    y0 from pyramidal output, y1 from excitatory input plus noise p(t), y2 from inhibition
EEG proxy ← y1 − y2;  spectrum ← |FFT(last 4 s)|²
