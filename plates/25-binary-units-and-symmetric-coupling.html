Binary Units and Symmetric Coupling

Rate Population Model → Attractor Network

An attractor network keeps one bit of each unit: on or off. That binary unit is the limit of a rate unit whose gain function is made ever steeper. As the sigmoid sharpens towards a step, every graded rate is pushed to its floor or its ceiling, and a network of graded units comes to behave like one of two-state units (Hopfield, 1984). Read at the level of a population, the bit says whether that population sits in its high or its low state.

Sixteen rate units each receive an input whose sign is set by a stored pattern and whose strength differs from unit to unit. The left panel places every input on the gain function; the right panel shows the resulting rates and the binary state each one rounds to. Raise the gain.

Which units are on never changes: that is fixed by the sign of the input, and it is what the attractor network stores in its symmetric weights, the subject of the next plate. What disappears as the gain rises is everything graded: how strongly each unit responds and how close it is to switching. The readout gives the graded part that remains, the average distance of the rates from 0 or 1.

Equations
ri = f(hi) = 1 / (1 + e−β hi)
si = limβ→∞ (2 ri − 1) = sign(hi)
Each unit receives hi = ai ξi, where ξi = ±1 is the stored pattern and ai > 0 a fixed input strength per unit.
Pseudocode
for each unit i:
    h_i ← a_i · ξ_i
    r_i ← 1 / (1 + exp(−β h_i))
    s_i ← +1 if r_i > ½, else −1
raise β: every r_i moves to 0 or 1, and r_i → (s_i + 1) / 2
