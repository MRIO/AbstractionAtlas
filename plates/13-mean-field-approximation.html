Mean-Field Approximation: From Spiking Networks to Rate Models

Spiking Network → Rate Population Model

A mean-field approximation replaces a population of spiking neurons with a single variable, its firing rate. The bridge between the two levels is the transfer function F, the rate at which a neuron fires for a given mean input and noise. If every neuron sees the population only through averages, the rate model needs F and nothing else.

The left panel overlays the binned rate of a spiking network, with excitatory and inhibitory populations coupled through their average activity, and the prediction of the two-population rate model, both driven by the same step in external input. Change the size of the network, the step and the noise each neuron receives; the right panel plots F with dots from the spiking simulation.

For a large network with enough noise the rate model follows the population closely, and the dots fall on the curve. A small network makes the population rate ragged, and without noise the identical neurons fire in lockstep, so the averaging that the rate model relies on no longer holds.

Population rate: spiking network and rate model

Transfer function

Keeps
Slow, population-wide dynamics: fixed points, oscillations and bifurcations.
Loses
Spike timing, synchrony between cells and the noise of finite populations.
Trust it when
Populations are large and their firing is asynchronous.
Ask yourself
Which result of the mean-field model would you expect to break in a network of only 50 neurons, and why?
Equations
spiking: τ dVj/dt = −Vj + μ(t) + σ √τ ξj(t),   spike and reset at θ
μE = μext + JEE sE − JIE sI,   μI = μext + JEI sE − JII sI
rate model: τs dsE/dt = −sE + F(μE, σ),   τs dsI/dt = −sI + F(μI, σ)
F(μ, σ) = [τref + τ √π ∫(Vr−μ)/σ(θ−μ)/σ eu²(1 + erf u) du]−1
In the spiking network sE and sI are the population spike counts per neuron and per second, smoothed with τs. θ = 20 mV, Vr = 10 mV, τ = 20 ms, τref = 2 ms, τs = 10 ms.
Pseudocode
spiking level, for each time step dt:
    every neuron integrates μ_ext + J·(smoothed population activity) + its own noise
    population activity ← (spikes in this step / N) / dt, smoothed with τ_s
rate level, for each time step dt:
    s_E ← s_E + dt/τ_s · (−s_E + F(μ_E, σ));  likewise for s_I
compare the binned spiking rate with s_E
