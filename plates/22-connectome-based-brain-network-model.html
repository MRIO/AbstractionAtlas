Connectome-Based Brain Network Model

whole brain · 0.1 to 10 s

A connectome-based model places one neural mass at each brain region and couples the regions through a structural connectome, the measured strength of the white-matter connections between them. Signals travel between regions with a delay set by distance and conduction speed.

Twenty regions in two hemispheres each hold an oscillator near the onset of its rhythm, driven by noise. Change the global coupling strength and the conduction speed; the right panel shows the functional connectivity, the correlation between regional activities, beside the structural matrix, with their similarity as a single number.

With weak coupling the regions fluctuate independently, and with strong coupling they lock together into one global rhythm. In between, the functional connectivity takes on the structure of the wiring without copying it, which is the regime these models are usually tuned to.

Ask yourself
Two regions with no direct connection can still be strongly correlated here. Through what could that correlation arise?
Equations
dzj/dt = (α + iωj) zj − |zj|² zj + G Σi Cij zi(t − dij/v) + η ξj(t)
activity xj = Re zj;   FCij = corr(xi, xj)
Each region is the normal form of a neural mass near the onset of its rhythm (α slightly below zero, frequencies near 10 Hz). Cij is the structural connection from region i to region j, dij their distance and v the conduction speed.
Pseudocode
for each time step dt:
    for each region j:
        delayed input ← Σ_i C_ij · z_i(t − d_ij / v)
        z_j ← z_j + dt · ((α + iω_j)·z_j − |z_j|²·z_j + G·delayed input) + noise
FC ← correlation matrix of Re z over 7 s, after a 1 s transient
similarity ← correlation between the off-diagonal entries of FC and C
