Phase Reduction

Neural Mass Model → Phase Oscillator Model

Phase reduction summarises an oscillator by a single number, its phase, the position along its limit cycle measured in time since a reference point. A weak input can only nudge the oscillator along the cycle, advancing or delaying it, and how much it does so at each phase is captured by the phase response curve.

The oscillating excitatory–inhibitory pair of the rate model runs on its cycle in the left panel. Choose where on the cycle a kick arrives and how strong it is; the right panel compares the phase shift it actually produces with the prediction of the phase response curve.

For weak kicks the measured shift sits on the curve, and the whole oscillator is well described by its phase and that curve. Strong kicks push the state far off the cycle, the amplitude that the reduction discards comes back into play, and the prediction fails.

Equations
dθ/dt = 1/T + Z(θ) I(t)
for a brief kick ε at phase θ: Δθ ≈ ε Z(θ)
θ runs from 0 to 1 over one period T; Z is the phase response curve for kicks to rE, measured here by kicking the full model with a small ε at many phases. The oscillator is the rate model with IE = 1.5 and wEI = 15.
Pseudocode
find the limit cycle and its period T
for each phase θ on the cycle:
    kick r_E by a small ε, run on for several periods
    Z(θ) ← (phase shift measured against the unkicked run) / ε
for the chosen kick: compare the measured shift with ε·Z(θ)
