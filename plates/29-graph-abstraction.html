Graph Abstraction

Large-Scale Network Model → Connectivity Graph

Graph abstraction keeps only the wiring of a network. Each neuron or population becomes a vertex and each connection an edge, possibly weighted, while activity, signs, delays and dynamics are set aside.

On the left a small network of rate units runs, with excitatory and inhibitory connections and activity shown by shading; on the right the same network is drawn as a graph. Raise the threshold below which weak edges are dropped and watch the apparent structure of the graph change.

The graph preserves who connects to whom and how strongly, and it can be analysed with tools that know nothing about neurons. It cannot say whether a connection excites or inhibits, or how activity flows over time, and the threshold, a choice made by the analyst, alters measures such as degree and clustering.

Equations
Aij = 1 if |wij| > θw, and 0 otherwise (direction and sign ignored)
degree ki = Σj Aij
clustering Ci = (edges among the neighbours of i) / (ki(ki − 1)/2)
The dynamics on the left: τ daj/dt = −aj + tanh(Σi wij ai + noise).
Pseudocode
for every pair of units (i, j):
    keep an undirected edge if |w_ij| or |w_ji| exceeds θ_w
draw the kept edges, with width ∝ |w|
compute degree, clustering and the number of connected components
