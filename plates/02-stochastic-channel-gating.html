Stochastic Channel Gating

≈ 10 nm · μs to ms

An ion channel is a membrane protein with a small number of conformations, and in the simplest description it has only two, closed and open. Transitions between them happen at random, with an opening rate α(V) and a closing rate β(V) that both depend on the membrane voltage. While a channel is open it passes a fixed current, so the current through a patch of membrane rises and falls in discrete steps of one single-channel current.

Each row of the left panel is one channel, and beneath the rows the summed current steps between multiples of the unitary current. Change the voltage and the channels spend more or less of their time open; add channels and the steps multiply. The right panel collects the duration of every opening.

Voltage changes the probability that a channel is open and the rates at which it switches, while the size of an opening stays the same. Open times scatter widely, and their histogram settles onto an exponential with mean 1/β, the signature of a memoryless transition between two states.

Ask yourself
In a very small cell only a handful of channels are open at rest. Could one random opening be enough to start a spike there, and what would that mean for the reliability of such a cell?
Equations
α(V) = k e(V − V½)/2s,   β(V) = k e−(V − V½)/2s
Popen = α/(α + β),   τ = 1/(α + β)
mean open time = 1/β,   mean closed time = 1/α
I = Nopen · γ · (V − E)
Parameters: k = 0.1 ms⁻¹, V½ = −40 mV, s = 8 mV, single-channel conductance γ = 10 pS, reversal potential E = +50 mV.
Pseudocode
for each time step dt:
    for each channel:
        if closed: open with probability α(V)·dt
        if open:   close with probability β(V)·dt
                   (when it closes, record how long it was open)
    I ← (number of open channels) · γ · (V − E)
