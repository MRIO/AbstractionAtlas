Excitatory–Inhibitory Rate Model

one population · ≈ 10 ms

An excitatory and an inhibitory population, each described by its firing rate, are coupled in a loop in which excitation drives both and inhibition holds excitation back. Each rate relaxes towards a sigmoidal function of its input, so the whole system lives in a plane with rE on one axis and rI on the other.

Change the external drive to the excitatory population and the strength wEI of the projection from excitation onto inhibition. Click in the plane to start from a different state, and compare the trajectory with the rates over time.

Depending on these two numbers the pair settles to a steady state, rests in one of two stable states, or oscillates on a limit cycle. The three regimes correspond to where the nullclines cross and whether those crossings are stable, one picture for behaviours that the spiking network showed only as patterns in a raster.

Equations
τE drE/dt = −rE + fE(wEE rE − wIE rI + IE)
τI drI/dt = −rI + fI(wEI rE − wII rI)
f(x) = 1/(1 + e−a(x − θ))
wij is the projection from population i to population j. wEE = 16, wIE = 12, wII = 3; aE = 1.3, θE = 4; aI = 2, θI = 3.7; τE = τI = 10 ms.
Pseudocode
choose a starting state (r_E, r_I)
for each time step dt:
    r_E ← r_E + dt/τ_E · (−r_E + f_E(w_EE·r_E − w_IE·r_I + I_E))
    r_I ← r_I + dt/τ_I · (−r_I + f_I(w_EI·r_E − w_II·r_I))
draw (r_E, r_I) in the plane, and both rates against time
