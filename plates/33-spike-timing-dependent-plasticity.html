Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity

Acts on Spiking Network, Rate Population Model, Artificial Neural Network, Attractor Network

Hebbian learning strengthens a synapse when the presynaptic and postsynaptic neurons are active together. Spike-timing-dependent plasticity makes the rule precise to the millisecond: the change in weight depends on the interval Δt = tpost − tpre between a presynaptic and a postsynaptic spike, through a learning window measured in experiments.

Drag the two spikes along their traces. The bracket shows Δt, the window on the right marks where that interval falls, and the synapse at the far right thickens or thins accordingly. Choose another window shape, and press the repeat button to apply the same pairing again and again.

In the asymmetric Hebbian window a presynaptic spike that precedes the postsynaptic one, Δt > 0, strengthens the synapse, and the reverse order weakens it. Other preparations show reversed or symmetric windows, so the same pair of spikes can strengthen one synapse and weaken another. Repeating a pairing turns a single point on the curve into learning.

Equations
Δt = tpost − tpre
asymmetric Hebbian: Δw = A+ e−Δt/τ+ for Δt > 0,   Δw = −A− eΔt/τ− for Δt < 0
asymmetric anti-Hebbian: the same window with the sign reversed
symmetric depression: Δw = −A e−|Δt|/τ
symmetric with depressing flanks: Δw = A (1 − Δt²/τ²) e−Δt²/2τ²
A+ = 0.08, A− = 0.045, τ+ = 17 ms, τ− = 34 ms; weights are kept between 0 and 1. The window shapes follow those reviewed by Abbott and Nelson (2000), to be checked against the review.
Pseudocode
for each pairing of a presynaptic and a postsynaptic spike:
    Δt ← t_post − t_pre
    Δw ← W(Δt)          (W is the chosen learning window)
    w ← clip(w + Δw, 0, 1)
