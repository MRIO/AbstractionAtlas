Noise-Enhanced Signal Detection

Acts on Ion Channels and Synapses, Spiking Network, Rate Population Model, Neural Field

A weak periodic signal that never reaches a neuron's threshold leaves no trace in its spikes. Adding noise changes this: the fluctuations carry the voltage across threshold most often near the peaks of the signal, so the spikes begin to follow it. The effect is known as stochastic resonance.

A subthreshold sine wave drives a leaky integrate-and-fire neuron together with noise of adjustable strength. The left panel shows the signal, the membrane voltage and the spikes; the right panel shows how faithfully the spikes follow the signal across noise levels, with a marker at the current setting.

Without noise there are no spikes and nothing is transmitted. A moderate amount of noise lines the spikes up with the peaks of the signal, while strong noise produces spikes at any phase and buries the signal again. Fidelity therefore peaks at an intermediate noise level, one reason why variability in neural systems can be useful.

Fidelity against noise

Ask yourself
Would adding noise help a signal that already crosses threshold on its own? What does that say about when noise can be useful?
Equations
τ dV/dt = −(V − EL) + R (I0 + I1 sin 2πft) + σ √τ ξ(t)
fidelity = correlation between the spike count in 20 ms bins and the signal
EL = −70 mV, θ = −50 mV, reset −70 mV, τ = 20 ms; RI0 = 16 mV and RI1 = 3 mV, so the noiseless voltage peaks about 1 mV below threshold; f = 5 Hz.
Pseudocode
for each time step dt:
    V ← V + dt/τ · (−(V − E_L) + R·(I_0 + I_1·sin 2πft)) + σ·√(dt/τ)·ξ
    if V ≥ θ: record a spike, V ← V_reset
fidelity ← correlation(spike counts in 20 ms bins, signal)
