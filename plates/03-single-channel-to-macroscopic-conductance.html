From Single-Channel Currents to Macroscopic Conductance

Ion Channels and Synapses → Compartmental Neuron

A patch of membrane holds many channels of the same kind, each gating independently with the rates of the previous plate. When the voltage steps, every channel changes its odds at once, and the fraction p of open channels relaxes towards a new equilibrium. The deterministic description replaces the count of open channels with that fraction, which obeys a single differential equation.

Both levels receive the same voltage step. With a few channels the stochastic trace jumps in coarse increments around the smooth curve; with more channels the jumps shrink until the two are hard to tell apart. Rerun the trial for a fresh realisation of the noise.

The size of the fluctuations falls as one over the square root of the number of channels, plotted on logarithmic axes in the right panel. The smooth conductance of the conductance-based neuron is a statement about large numbers, exact only in the limit, and its error at any finite size can be read off that curve.

Equations
dp/dt = α(V)(1 − p) − β(V) p
p(t) = p∞ + (p0 − p∞) e−t/τ,   p∞ = α/(α + β),   τ = 1/(α + β)
Var[nopen/N] = p(1 − p)/N,   so the standard deviation falls as 1/√N
Pseudocode
apply the voltage step at t = t_step
for each time step dt:
    stochastic:    n_open ← n_open + Binomial(N − n_open, α·dt) − Binomial(n_open, β·dt)
    deterministic: p ← p + dt · (α·(1 − p) − β·p)
plot n_open/N and p on the same axes
fluctuation size ← standard deviation of n_open/N after the relaxation
