Coupled Phase Oscillators

Coupled phase oscillators reduce every member of a population to a phase that advances at its own natural frequency and is pulled towards the phases of the others. Synchrony is measured by the order parameter r, the length of the average of all phases taken as points on the unit circle: near zero for scattered phases and one for perfect alignment.

Thirty oscillators circle on the left, each with its own natural frequency, and the right panel follows r over time. Change the coupling strength K and the spread of natural frequencies.

Below a critical coupling the oscillators drift past one another and r stays small. Above it a cluster forms and grows, and the transition is sharp: synchrony appears once the coupling overcomes the spread of frequencies, at a critical value proportional to that spread.

Ask yourself
Why does the critical coupling depend on the spread of natural frequencies and not on their average?
Equations
dθj/dt = ωj + (K/N) Σi sin(θi − θj)
r eiψ = (1/N) Σj eiθj
for a Gaussian spread σ of natural frequencies: Kc = 2/(π g(0)) = √(8/π) σ ≈ 1.6 σ
Pseudocode
for each time step dt:
    for each oscillator j:
        pull ← (K/N) · Σ_i sin(θ_i − θ_j)
        θ_j ← θ_j + dt · (ω_j + pull)
    r ← |mean of e^{iθ_j}|
