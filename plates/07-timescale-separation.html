Timescale Separation

Conductance-Based Point Neuron → Reduced Neuron Models

When some variables of a model change much faster than others, the fast ones can be replaced by the values they would settle at, and slow ones that move together can be tied to each other. This is timescale separation, and in the conductance-based neuron it reduces four equations to two while keeping the spike.

Choose a stage of the reduction and compare the reduced model with the full one on the same current. At the first stage sodium activation m is set to its steady state at every instant; at the second, sodium inactivation h is tied to potassium activation n by h ≈ 0.8 − n. The right panel shows the time constants that justify each step.

Sodium activation is roughly ten times faster than the other gates, which is why replacing it costs so little, and h and n move in near opposition during a spike, which is why one can stand in for the other. Each stage keeps the spike and its threshold while the waveform drifts slightly, and at two variables the model can be drawn completely in a plane, the subject of the next node.
